VOCALISCOPE: Evidence-Based Contrastive Speech Analytics & Temporal Flaw Grounding

Engineering Team: Vocaliscope Research Team  |  Artifact: Technical Submission Report (Max 6 Pages)  |  Repository: github.com/vocaliscope/vocaliscope
Abstract: Automated presentation and speech evaluation systems frequently suffer from black-box subjectivity, hallucinated ratings, and lack of temporal grounding. Vocaliscope introduces an evidence-first, contrastive acoustic speech evaluation workstation that grounds speech flaws to exact millisecond intervals with physical signal measurements. Built around a strictly deterministic, bounded 0–100 rubric spanning four acoustic dimensions (Cadence & Pacing, Pause Discipline, Pitch Variation, and Energy Dynamics), Vocaliscope replaces opaque LLM judgements with verifiable acoustic features (short-time frame RMS, YIN fundamental frequency contours, and Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients). Using a controlled demonstration dataset with word-for-word ground truth transcripts and flaw boundary labels, we demonstrate that Vocaliscope reliably flags mid-clause hesitations, articulation stumbles, and pacing surges without making unsubstantiated claims regarding speaker emotion, linguistic semantics, or rhetorical truth.

1. Problem Formulation & System Overview

In high-stakes communications, speakers struggle with subconscious delivery flaws: unmetered speech acceleration, disfluent mid-clause breaks, and monotone inflections. Existing commercial tools report generalized, ungrounded summary scores (e.g. "Pacing: 7/10") without pinpointing where the breakdown occurred or explaining the physical cause. Track C requires:

  • Temporal Flaw Grounding: Isolating exact start/end timestamps of delivery deviations.
  • Causal Acoustic Rationale: Quantifying physical deviations (RMS drops, $F_0$ invariance, active bursts) linked to delivery context.
  • Contrastive Spectrum: Benchmarking perturbed deliveries against calibrated baselines using controlled scripts.
  • Mathematical Determinism: Ensuring evaluation rubrics are 100% reproducible across repeated runs.

2. Advanced Acoustic Feature Extraction Engine

Vocaliscope executes signal extraction in Python using librosa, NumPy, and soundfile, preceded by automated audio extraction via imageio-ffmpeg.

2.1 Signal Ingestion and Standardized Preprocessing

Audio signals are accepted in seven standard container formats (.wav, .mp3, .m4a, .flac, .ogg, .webm, .mp4) up to 25 MB. Every file is downmixed to mono, resampled to $f_s = 22,050\text{ Hz}$, and normalized into 16-bit linear PCM. This standardization neutralizes container codec artifacts, multi-channel panning bias, and variable sample rate skew.

2.2 Short-Time Frame RMS Energy Gating

Energy envelopes are computed over centered windows using a frame length $N = 2048$ samples ($\sim 92.9\text{ ms}$) and hop length $H = 512$ samples ($\sim 23.2\text{ ms}$):

\text{RMS}[m] = \sqrt{ \frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} \left( y[m \cdot H + n] \cdot w[n] \right)^2 }

To classify active speech frames versus acoustic attenuation without making unwarranted phonetic claims, Vocaliscope establishes an empirical recording-relative energy gate:

\tau_{\text{rel}} = \max\left(0.05 \cdot \max_{m}(\text{RMS}[m]),\; 10^{-6}\right)

Frames where $\text{RMS}[m] > \tau_{\text{rel}}$ are tagged as active acoustic energy; contiguous runs below $\tau_{\text{rel}}$ are registered as low-energy intervals.

2.3 Bounded Fundamental Frequency ($F_0$) Contour via YIN

Fundamental frequency estimation employs the YIN algorithm (de Cheveigné & Kawahara, 2002) over the vocal search range $[f_{\min}, f_{\max}] = [65\text{ Hz}, 400\text{ Hz}]$. YIN minimizes the cumulative mean normalized difference function to identify periodic pitch cycles:

d_t(\tau) = \sum_{j=1}^{W} (x_j - x_{j+\tau})^2, \quad d'_t(\tau) = \begin{cases} 1 & \tau = 0 \\ \frac{d_t(\tau)}{\frac{1}{\tau} \sum_{j=1}^{\tau} d_t(j)} & \tau > 0 \end{cases}

From valid voiced frames ($65 \le F_0 \le 400\text{ Hz}$), we compute the median $F_0$, the 10th percentile floor ($P_{10}$), and the 90th percentile ceiling ($P_{90}$).

2.4 Spectral Characterization via 13-Coefficient MFCCs

Vocal timbre and spectral envelope dynamics are captured using 13 Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients computed over 40 triangular filter banks spanning $0$ to $f_s/2 = 11,025\text{ Hz}$. Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT-II) transforms log filter-bank energies into orthogonal cepstral coefficients, providing an acoustic timbre baseline invariant to linguistic content.

3. Deterministic Rubric Scoring Methodology

To fulfill Track C's reproducibility mandate, Vocaliscope implements an open, mathematically bounded scoring engine in backend/rubric.py. The rubric scores recordings on a normalized $0 - 100$ scale across four independent acoustic dimensions, combined via explicit linear weighting.

Dimension 1: Cadence & Pacing ($W_1 = 30\%$)

Measures the ratio of active speech frames to total duration: $R_{\text{act}} = T_{\text{active}} / T_{\text{total}}$. Communicative speech requires a natural $18\% - 28\%$ pause cushion for clause breathing.

S_1 = \max(15, \min(100, 100 - 135 \cdot |R_{\text{act}} - 0.77|))

Penalizes hurried speech ($R_{\text{act}} > 0.85$) and sparse/fragmented delivery ($R_{\text{act}} < 0.65$).

Dimension 2: Pause Discipline ($W_2 = 25\%$)

Evaluates pause frequency and hesitation penalties. Penalizes prolonged mid-utterance silence gaps ($d_i \ge 750\text{ ms}$) and excessive fragmentation:

S_2 = 94 - 110 \cdot \Delta_{\text{sil}} - 6.5 \cdot N_{\text{hes}} - 4.0 \cdot \Delta_{\text{frag}}

Where $N_{\text{hes}}$ is the count of hesitations $\ge 0.75\text{s}$ and $\Delta_{\text{sil}}$ is silence ratio error.

Dimension 3: Pitch Variation ($W_3 = 25\%$)

Calculates $F_0$ dynamic range in semitones: $\Delta_{\text{st}} = 12 \log_2(P_{90} / P_{10})$. Healthy speech exhibits $6 - 16$ semitones of inflection.

S_3 = \begin{cases} 45 + 7.0 \cdot \Delta_{\text{st}} & \Delta_{\text{st}} < 5.0\text{ (Monotone)} \\ 85 + 1.36(\Delta_{\text{st}} - 5) & 5 \le \Delta_{\text{st}} \le 16 \\ 100 - 3.5(\Delta_{\text{st}} - 16) & \Delta_{\text{st}} > 16\text{ (Erratic)} \end{cases}

Dimension 4: Energy Dynamics ($W_4 = 20\%$)

Evaluates mean frame RMS energy stability against calibrated vocal projection range ($0.04 - 0.15\text{ RMS}$):

S_4 = \max(25, \min(100, 94 - 32 \cdot |\log_{10}(\bar{E}_{\text{rms}} / 0.065)|))

Faint audio ($<0.003$) scores $20$; digital clipping risks ($>0.35$) score $45$.

3.1 Composite Score Formulation & Performance Bands

\text{Composite Score} = 0.30 \cdot S_1 + 0.25 \cdot S_2 + 0.25 \cdot S_3 + 0.20 \cdot S_4

Scores map deterministically into four standardized performance tiers:

Score Range Classification Band Acoustic Delivery Characteristics
85.0 – 100.0 Exemplary Optimal active ratio ($72-82\%$), clause-aligned pauses, dynamic pitch modulation ($6-16\text{ st}$), steady vocal projection.
70.0 – 84.9 Proficient Clear delivery with minor tempo variance or occasional hesitation pauses; satisfactory dynamic range.
55.0 – 69.9 Developing Notable pacing irregularities, compressed syllable bursts, prolonged hesitation gaps, or monotone pitch tendency.
0.0 – 54.9 Needs Attention Severe pacing imbalance ($>88\%$ or $<60\%$), extreme hesitation fragmentation, flat pitch ($<3\text{ st}$), or inaudible energy.

3.2 Mathematical Determinism & Reproducibility Guarantee

Unlike stochastic generative LLM judges, Vocaliscope's rubric contains zero non-deterministic components, zero temperature parameters, and zero random seeds. Processing the same audio file $N$ times produces bit-for-bit identical dimensional and composite scores (verified by tests/test_rubric.py::test_reproducibility_deterministic).

4. Temporal Flaw Grounding Architecture

Track C's defining mandate is grounding speech flaws to exact time ranges with causal explanations. Vocaliscope detects four physical flaw event classes:

4.1 Interactive SVG Timeline & Micro-Inspection Modal

The frontend rendering engine (frontend/src/components/Timeline.tsx) translates grounded flaw intervals into an interactive SVG coordinate track. Interval bars are color-coded (Cyan for natural pauses, Amber for hesitations, Coral for pacing surges, Violet for pitch monotony) with hover playhead guides and full keyboard navigation (Tab/Enter/Space). Clicking any event invokes EventDetail.tsx, presenting exact millisecond boundaries, duration, threshold ratios, and measurement limitations.

6. Evidence Workstation, Limitations & Compliance

6.1 Signal Noir × Chroma Sonar Frontend Workstation

Built with React 19, TypeScript, and Vite 8, the frontend provides an accessible dark-canvas aesthetic with high-contrast acoustic data visualization:

  • Single-Recording View: Overview metrics, pitch contour charts, interactive SVG timeline, and the complete 4-dimension rubric scorecard.
  • Side-by-Side Comparison: Contrastive tabs displaying metric differences, pitch distributions, synchronized timelines, and rubric deltas.
  • Real-Time Voice Assistant: Accessible via mic button or spacebar hold, powered by Web Speech API, answering user queries strictly with computed acoustic measurements.

6.2 Explicit Scientific Limitations & Ethical Boundaries

Scientific Boundary Disclosures:
  • No Rhetorical or Emotional Inference: Physical acoustic measurements (RMS, $F_0$, MFCCs) reflect physical sound properties. Vocaliscope does not claim to measure persuasion, truthfulness, grammatical accuracy, or speaker emotion.
  • No Word Alignment: In the absence of heavy ASR forced-alignment dependencies, temporal flaw regions are extracted from short-time acoustic energy and frequency frames.
  • Dataset Scope: The bundled demonstration dataset ($N=2$ recordings from a single speaker) serves as a controlled reference harness, not a generalized multi-speaker population standard.
